0. Summary card

Mego! (FR company) converts collected cigarette butts into clean fibrous cellulose acetate (CA) fine particles, and optionally thermo-compresses them into CA plates for handling pallets and urban furniture. Mechanism: continuous grinding and multi-level sieving separate the filter from organic material; density separation in a water wash tank floats CA off from paper; three decontamination tanks in series wash out water-soluble toxins; a clarifloculation recycler treats up to 1000 L of wash water per 2-hour cycle; drying at 100-120 degC; second milling to <1500 um particles. Priority because it is the only butt-recycling patent on our registry whose granted specification discloses a near-complete numeric protocol, because it uses water (cheap, low-hazard) rather than solvents, and because its plate-making variant gives a product the team could plausibly form at pilot scale. Legal: granted FR patent, active, expires 2037-05-15 — commercial operation in France is blocked until expiry; lab verification and non-commercial research are outside the patent's prohibition scope only to the extent French law allows; verify before any use. 1

1. What the sources disclose — step by step

All page references are to the granted B1 specification text on the shelf (sources/fr3066128a1-granted/text.md). Steps 20 and 21 appear in the figure-2 embodiment.

  1. Collection & storage (20) — butts are collected selectively, in containers that receive only butts, then shipped to the valorisation site for storage. 1
  2. Manual triage (21) — manual removal of foreign objects: chewing gum, matches, aluminium foil from packets, anything that is not a butt. 1
  3. First grinding (22) — butts pass in continuous flow through a first mill that dices them at 1 kg of butts per minute; the filter is separated from the organic material (ash, paper leaves, tobacco). 1
  4. Sieving (23) — the ground stream goes to a vibrating sieve (motor-driven), separating material over a three-level gradient, at 1 kg/min; the filter fraction is recovered; the organic material is stored and later partially depolluted in a water bath (not further specified). 1 sieve mesh sizes / aperture of the three levels (evidence: parameter not located)
  5. Filter wash (24) — wash tank with water as solvent (acetone named as an alternative in one embodiment); separation is by density: paper (higher density) sinks to the tank floor, CA floats at the surface; duration at least 20 minutes, preferably 30 minutes; sunken paper is removed and sent to paper recycling. 1 Note an erratum in the spec: one sentence labels paper as (41), the filter's numeral — the intended numeral is (34). 1 water volume per batch, water temperature, agitation (evidence: parameter not located)
  6. Decontamination wash (25) — decontamination tank with water; closed-flow technique; nicotine is stated to dissolve in water; CA stays at least 20 minutes, preferably 30 minutes. The operation is repeated three times, in three decontamination tanks in series with pump transfer between tanks (fig. 3 embodiment). The spec explicitly admits some residues such as tar need another type of solvent that water does not provide. 1 water volume per tank, temperature, replacement rate of water (evidence: parameter not located)
  7. Water recycling (26) — a recycler applies clarifloculation: coagulation (destabilisation of colloids) then flocculation (micro-floc growth to settleable flocs) then settling; treats up to 1000 litres per cycle, each cycle about 2 hours, energy 1 kW so about 2 kWh per cycle; recycled water is stored in tank (36) and returned to the wash and decontamination tanks. 1
  8. Drying by pre-cooking (27) — pre-cooking oven, at least 100 degC, preferably 120 degC; the step ends when no more water vapour appears at the air exhaust. 1 residual moisture target, batch depth/tray loading (evidence: parameter not located)
  9. Second milling (28) — dried CA milled to fibrous fine particles of diameter below 1500 um, preferably close to 1000 um; step ends when the CA is shredded to fine particles. 1 particle size distribution width, fines fraction (evidence: parameter not located)
  10. Thermo-compression (29, optional step h) — the particles are melted in a hydraulic thermo-compression press into CA plates of chosen thickness: 314 tonnes press force, 80 x 60 cm working surface, heating at 350 degC; combined heating and cooling stations; automatic programmable cycle; mechanical stops on the two rams give precise thickness. Plates enable pallets, urban furniture, under-glass, flooring, advertising supports, outdoor ashtrays, flower pots. 1 dwell time, cooling time, pressure per unit area (evidence: parameter not located)

No yield, purity percentage, or contaminant-removal efficiency is disclosed anywhere in the specification — only qualitative statements ("quantities of harmful polluting substances decrease drastically"). 1

2. Equipment

  • First mill, continuous feed, ~1 kg/min throughput at pilot scale 1
  • Vibrating sieve with motor, three-level gradient 1
  • Wash tank (water), with paper-removal means at the bottom 1
  • Three decontamination tanks in series + transfer pump 1
  • Clarifloculation water recycler, <=1000 L/cycle, ~2 h/cycle, ~1 kW; recycled-water storage tank 1
  • Pre-cooking oven >=100-120 degC with air exhaust 1
  • Second mill to <1500 um 1
  • Optional: hydraulic thermo-compression press, 314 t, 80 x 60 cm platens, 350 degC heating station + cooling station 1

Scale note: the spec describes an industrial pilot line (kg/min). A student-lab replication needs miniaturised equivalents (bench grinder or knife mill, laboratory sieve shaker, 20-50 L tanks, small clarifloculation unit or batch water replacement, forced-air oven, bench mill). The 314 t press is not a student-lab item; a 10-30 t lab press at 350 degC is the closest faithful analogue.

3. Gap-fill table — labeled expert estimates

# Gap Estimate (labeled) Reasoning Falsifier — how the team tests it
1 Wash / decontamination water temperature ai proposal: wash tank 40-50 degC; decontamination tanks ambient 20-25 degC Wash temperature is nowhere disclosed. De Fenzo's experimentally supported hot-water stage is 50 degC for 60 min 3, so 40-50 degC is a validated leaching temperature for water-soluble toxins; ambient decontamination tanks save energy and the 3x repetition compensates. Run 1 kg test batches: 20 min ambient vs 40 degC vs 50 degC wash; measure nicotine in spent wash water (HPLC). If ambient removes as much as 50 degC, drop the heating cost.
2 Water volume per kg of butts ai inference: ~8.3 L per kg of butts processed (computed) Computed from disclosed numbers: recycler treats 1000 L per ~2 h cycle 1; line feeds 1 kg/min 1 = 60 kg/h, so 120 kg per cycle; 1000/120 = 8.33 L/kg. A 10 kg lab batch needs ~83 L total wash water. Mass-flow the pilot: log make-up water per kg processed. If the recycler cannot keep up at 1 kg/min, the patent's own throughput is inconsistent — kill this estimate and downrate the patent's feasibility.
3 Paper-to-CA separation efficiency (single 30-min wash) ai proposal: >=90% of paper mass sinks and is removed in one pass; >=98% after a second pass Physics: water-soaked paper (bulk density ~1.2 g/cm3 wet) sinks; CA tow traps air and floats. No efficiency is disclosed 1. Weigh the sunken paper fraction vs residual paper visible in the floated CA (30x magnification image analysis) on a 1 kg batch; acceptance: <10% residual paper mass fraction in floated CA after pass 1.
4 CA yield (dried fine particles per kg of butts) ai inference: 45-60% of input butt mass ends as dried CA particles Reasoning chain: the product is the filter's CA only 1; organic material is removed upstream (step 3-4); paper is removed by sinking (step 5); filter tow dominates butt mass and CA dominates the tow. Cross-check: De Fenzo's recovered CA shows ~10% residue at TGA end 3, consistent with mostly-polymer product. Weigh dried product from a 1 kg pilot run. Acceptance band 40-70%; outside the band, re-derive from the measured filter/organic split at step 4 instead of this estimate.
5 Nicotine removal across 3 tanks parameter not located in 1 (only "decrease drastically"); working estimate ai proposal: 3 tanks x 30 min remove >=90% of extractable nicotine Nicotine is highly water-soluble; successive tanks keep a concentration gradient if tank water is partially refreshed. Nicotine assay (GC-FID or HPLC) of spent liquor from tank 1, 2, 3 and of a methanol extract of the final CA. Decision rule: <90% removal -> add a 4th tank or a fresh-water final rinse.
6 Tar / PAH removal 1 admits water does not remove tar ("another type of solvent" needed) 1; ai proposal: insert a final ethanol 99% w/w stage, 30 min, ambient, after tank 3 Transplanted from 3, where ethanol stages are part of an experimentally validated wash sequence on the same feedstock. PAH screen (16 US-EPA PAHs) on product before and after the ethanol stage. Acceptance: >=50% reduction; else the ethanol stage is not earning its solvent cost.
7 Recycled-water quality drift ai proposal: cap nicotine in the recycled-water tank at 50 mg/L; above it, bleed 20% to waste and add fresh make-up Closed loop 1 concentrates dissolved toxins over cycles, which weakens the leaching gradient the process relies on. The patent does not discuss this. Measure COD + nicotine in tank (36) every cycle for 5 cycles. Acceptance: values plateau; monotonic rise past the cap proves the closed loop degrades wash performance.
8 Drying endpoint (residual moisture) ai proposal: <=5% w/w residual moisture Disclosed endpoint is qualitative (no more steam at exhaust) 1; 5% is a conventional storage-safe moisture for polymer fluff. Gravimetric moisture test (105 degC to constant mass) per batch; acceptance <=5%, else extend drying.
9 Particle size distribution after second milling ai proposal: D90 <=1000 um with <10% mass below 100 um (fines) Spec targets <1500 um, pref. ~1000 um 1; the fines bound is added for dust-explosion and handling safety, not from the source. Sieve analysis (or laser diffraction) per batch; acceptance as stated; excess fines -> adjust mill screen.
10 Thermo-compression dwell ai proposal: 5-10 min at 350 degC for 3-5 mm plates Press force, platen size and heating temperature are disclosed 1; dwell is not. Range from ordinary hydraulic hot-pressing practice for acetate/polymer fluff. Press plates at 3, 7, 10 min; inspect cross-sections for voids/ unmelted particles; acceptance: void-free at the shortest dwell.
11 Metals in product (Cd, Pb, As, Hg) parameter not located — the patent never measures metals; stop-condition: route on hold for product-facing work until the team characterises feedstock metals Metals class is documented in butts 2; a water wash does not remove bound metals. ICP-MS on dried product + spent washes + recycler sludge. Until passed, no food-contact, no construction-material claims, no product distribution.

4. Safety notes

  • Grinding / sieving (steps 3-4): dust carries nicotine and combustion residues; enclose the mill, local exhaust, nitrile gloves + FFP3 mask. 1
  • Wash and decontamination tanks (steps 5-6): spent water is hazardous wastewater (nicotine, phenols, PAHs, carbonyls per 3; metals class per 2). Never to drain; licensed disposal or the clarifloculation step; sludge from the recycler concentrates the toxins — hazardous waste stream of its own.
  • Ethanol / acetone variants (gap rows 6, and 1 acetone embodiments): both solvents are highly flammable; fume hood, no ignition sources, grounded equipment.
  • Drying oven (step 8): 120 degC with residual organics; ventilated oven, do not exceed the disclosed range without a fire assessment.
  • Second milling (step 9): dry organic dust <1500 um is an explosible dust class; grounded mill, no ignition sources, avoid fines accumulation (row 9 bound exists for this reason).
  • Thermo-compression (step 10): 350 degC platens and a 314 t press are industrial hazards; qualified operators only, guarding, two-person rule. Not a student-lab step without a lab-grade press and risk assessment.
  • Material stop-condition: Cd/Pb/As/Hg uncharacterised in the feedstock means the route is on hold (row 11) — binds all downstream product work.
  • Desk research authorises no physical trial: every step above needs a qualified facility, a risk assessment, lawful waste handling, and a named human actor before it runs.

5. What the team must verify in lab

Ordered by information value:

  1. 1 kg mass-balance pilot (steps 3-9 miniaturised): throughput per step, dried-product mass. Acceptance: yield lands in the 40-70% band. Confirms/kills row 4; feeds row 2 with real water use.
  2. Nicotine removal assay across the three tanks (spent liquors + final CA extract). Acceptance: >=90% removal. Confirms/kills row 5; if it fails, the patent's core decontamination claim is materially weaker than stated.
  3. Water-recycle drift test (5 recycler cycles, COD + nicotine in tank 36). Acceptance: plateau below the 50 mg/L cap. Confirms/kills row 7 — and with it the patent's closed-loop economy.
  4. Paper-separation efficiency (1 kg batch, weigh sunken fraction, image-analysis of floated CA). Acceptance: <10% residual paper after one pass. Confirms/kills row 3.
  5. PAH screen before/after the proposed ethanol stage. Acceptance: >=50% reduction. Confirms/kills row 6.
  6. Product QC: moisture (row 8) + particle size distribution (row 9) + ICP-MS metals (row 11, gate).
  7. Only after 1-6: lab-press plates at 350 degC, 3/7/10 min dwell (row 10).
