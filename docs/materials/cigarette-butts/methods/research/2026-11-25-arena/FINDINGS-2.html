Second pass — unprompted findings, 2026-11-25

Things the brief didn't ask for that change how the stream should be modelled. Three items here correct or overturn conclusions in the first-pass report; those are marked ⚠️ CORRECTION at the top so they don't get read as new-and-additive.

Captures added to new-25-11-2026/ (3 more HTML files + 1 CSV). Bundle now 13 HTML/PDF + 3 CSV.

⚠️ CORRECTION 1 — the sterilization step comes FIRST after all. My first-pass inference was wrong.

In the first pass I inferred from Albe Zakes' 2013 quote that the order was shred → compost → irradiate the residual butts, and flagged that as a meaningful change to M-06 row 1. That was wrong, and I should have caught it — the Times of Trenton source in your own brief names a better witness than a press quote.

TerraCycle's Global VP of Research & Development — Ernie Simpson, the same person your brief already lists as the process developer — states the order unambiguously:

FACT | "Once collected, the cigarette butts go through a multi-step process developed by the company to separate the plastic in the butts from the toxins… 'We first sterilize the material with gamma, or irradiation sterilization, and then we separate the tobacco and paper from the filaments,' he says. 'Basically, we're looking for the cellulose acetate.'" — Ernie Simpson, Global VP R&D, TerraCycle, in CityLab, 2016-03-23 https://www.citylab.com/life/2016/03/can-turning-cigarette-butts-into-plastic-solve-the-litter-problem-terracycle/475044/ (live URL now redirects to a Bloomberg paywall/robot wall — captured via Wayback 2020-01-05, CityLab_2016-03-23_Simpson_sterilize-first_1000butts_100g_plastic.html) | confidence: high

So the tally is now 2 sources for sterilize-first vs 1 against:

  • Surfrider SF FAQ, 2017 — "first sterilized using Gamma radiation … then shredded" ✓ first
  • Simpson, TerraCycle R&D VP, 2016 — "we first sterilize … and then we separate" ✓ first
  • Zakes, TerraCycle Global VP, 2013 — gamma described after composting ✗

What this changes for M-06 row 1: the irradiation load is the whole intake — tobacco, ash, paper, wrapper and filter together, as received — not just the recovered filter fraction. That is a materially larger and more expensive step than my first-pass note implied, and it moves the utility/cost model back toward the "sterilize the bulk stream" assumption. The R&D VP outranks the comms VP; treat 2013 as the loose paraphrase. M-06 row 1 should read: whole-stream gamma sterilization upstream of mechanical separation.

Also from the same source — the reassuring part for modelling: the step is described as existing to "separate the plastic … from the toxins," i.e. a regulatory/hygiene requirement, not a materials one.

⚠️ CORRECTION 2 — the mass divisor is settled, and three independent routes agree to the gram

First pass I flagged "1 lb ≈ 2,268 butts, official figure says 1,000" and left it as an open tension. It is no longer open. Three unrelated routes converge on 0.20 g per smoked butt:

Route Evidence Implied
Composition (peer-reviewed) filter ≈ 0.20 g, 97% cellulose acetate → 0.194 g CA/butt 2,268 butts/lb
Weighed (field) "6.43 pounds … 14,583 cigarette butts" — Marquette, 2022 2,268 butts/lb
Plastic yield (TerraCycle's own) "about a thousand cigarette butts to produce 100 grams of plastic" 2,268 butts/lb

The third one is the elegant one: 1,000 butts × 0.194 g CA = 194 g of acetate in; 100 g of pellet out = ~52% mass yield, which is exactly what a mechanical recovery with losses should give. The number only coheres if a butt weighs 0.20 g.

FACT | "It takes about a thousand cigarette butts to produce 100 grams of plastic, which means it's not particularly profitable." — CityLab, 2016-03-23 (same article) | high

The official "1 lb = 1,000 butts" divisor implies 0.454 g/butt — over twice the true mass. It is an accounting convention, not a measurement, and it is self-consistent within TerraCycle's books (Carnival: 42.5M butts ÷ $42,500 = exactly 1,000/lb). So the reports are not lying about the donation — they are converting butts to pounds with a 2.3× inflator.

Consequences to carry into M-06 row 5:

Quantity As published Corrected (0.20 g/butt)
261,000 lb (KAB 2024) 118.4 t ~52 t
100M pieces (Dec 2018) 45.4 t 20.0 t
45,089,591 units (Dec 2015) 20.5 t 9.0 t
150,000 butts/pallet 150 lb of butts 66 lb of butts
Plastic yield — ~0.1 kg CA per 1,000 butts; ~50% mass yield to pellet

Recommendation now firmer than in pass 1: quote ~52 t as the real mass and ~118 t as the published figure, and state the divisor discrepancy explicitly. Reporting 118 t without this caveat would overstate the material by 2.3×.

This also kills the 5,000-butts-per-pallet figure from pass 1 more decisively: 5,000 butts ≈ 1 kg of filter, which cannot be a pallet. 150,000 butts ≈ 30 kg of filter into a pallet is right.

⚠️ CORRECTION 3 — your brief has the lawsuit wrong, and it matters

The brief says "Jan Dell false-advertising suit (settled 2021) … complaints/answers sometimes describe the process chain including sterilization," and names it as a place to hunt for gamma detail.

The case is Last Beach Cleanup v. TerraCycle, Inc., N.D. Cal. 4:21-cv-06086, filed 2021-03-04, removed 2021-08-06, settled 2021-11-05, dismissal with prejudice 2021-11-10. https://climatecasechart.com/case/last-beach-cleanup-v-terracycle-inc/ | confidence: high

It is not a cigarette case. The eight co-defendants are Coca-Cola, P&G, Late July Snacks, Gerber, L'Oréal, Tom's of Maine, Clorox and Materne — and the challenged claims are about packaging recyclability (condiment packets, pouches, cups). Cigarettes are not in the complaint, so it will never yield a cigarette-process description. Cross that item off the Wish 2 list.

What it does yield is a governance fact worth having: TerraCycle agreed to maintain records substantiating its recycling representations, develop policies to track materials for recycling, and implement a supply-chain third-party certification program, plus reimburse LBC's legal fees. If TerraCycle ever has to produce a per-stream mass ledger — for its own certification programme — that is the document to subpoena/request. It is the most plausible route to a weighed tonnage figure. That is a stronger lead than anything left in the Wish 1 list.

(The Bloomington, Illinois suit vs Bell International/Akshar is a separate, unrelated nuisance case about a warehouse; it will not describe sterilization either.)

Bonus 1 — market size and capture rate: the most useful number in this whole exercise

For the invention phase the relevant question isn't how much TerraCycle moved, it's what share of the problem that is. Nobody in the corpus states it, so here it is:

  • US butt waste: ~135 million lb/yr (≈ 61,000 t/yr), the figure TerraCycle and AP releases cite (see social.terracycle.com "Reynolds subsidiary funding cigarette recycling"). Sanity check: ~200 bn cigarettes/yr × 0.20 g ≈ 40,000 t = 88 M lb. Same order; 135 M lb is defensible with a smoking-rate-heavy vintage. Treat as order-of-magnitude, not precise.

  • TerraCycle: 261,000 lb cumulative over ~12 yr ≈ 21,750 lb/yr.

≈ 0.016% of US annual butt mass — about 1 pound in 6,200.

Even allowing a 2.3× divisor error in TerraCycle's favour or against it, this stays a hundredth-of-a-percent solution. Global: cigarette-butt litter is put at 1.69 billion lb/yr.

Why this is the useful number: it reframes M-06. The mail-back model is not a recycling pathway that happens to be small; it is a demonstration/PR instrument whose tonnage is rounding error against the stream. Any invention-phase design that inherits the collection assumption inherits that 0.016%. The binding constraint is collection and logistics, not the gamma step, not the pelletizing chemistry — those are solved and known. Budget and modelling effort should sit on collection density and consolidation, where the 2.3× divisor never mattered.

Corollary for the economics: at a $1/lb donation, the entire 12-year programme generated ~$261,000 total for Keep America Beautiful, against $12.5 million Reynolds says it has spent since 2009 on litter education, prevention, cleanup and recycling through KAB+TerraCycle. The recycling tail is ~2% of the spend; the programme is overwhelmingly a litter-prevention and marketing vehicle. Do not model it as a materials business.

Bonus 3 — patents: TerraCycle has none, which is itself the finding

Google Patents assignee search for TerraCycle: 0 results. TerraCycle has not patented the cigarette process — it is a trade secret. That explains, at a stroke, why the offering circular, the 1-K filings and the website all describe the process in one vague sentence and no parameters: there is no patent disclosure to mine, and the company has no incentive to publish.

The freedom-to-operate landscape around it, if the invention phase needs it:

  • US 7,560,059 B2 — "Process and method for recycling cigarette butts", Blake Burich, 2009. The core prior art; acetone-wetting of cellulose acetate.

  • US 2014/0287144 A1 — Burich & Putnam, CA manufacturing-waste recycling, 2014 (abandoned). Discloses the useful mechanical recipe: grind to ~½ inch, wet with acetone to a paste, add plasticizer/colorant/UV stabiliser, pelletize for injection or extrusion.

  • US 10,980,272 B2 — MINIWIZ Co., Ltd., granted 2021-04-20, "Method and apparatus for recycling cigarette waste." The most relevant live patent: a machine that aligns each butt, cuts out the non-filter part, then mechanically pushes the CA plug out of the paper. Explicitly discards non-CA filter blocks (activated carbon, PP, PLA). Also sets a 1.5 cm minimum butt length below which a butt is rejected as not worth recycling — a directly useful design parameter. https://patents.justia.com/patent/10980272

  • Nothing patents gamma irradiation of cigarette waste. Searches for cigarette + gamma/sterilization return only unrelated medical-device art. So the irradiation step is unclaimed and unparameterised in the patent literature — consistent with it being a contracted service under ISO 11137, not IP.

Bonus 4 — other parameters worth having

  • Filter composition, for mass balance: ~95–97% cellulose acetate of filter mass; 12,000 fibres per filter; plasticiser is triacetin (glycerol triacetate) at 6–10% mass fraction; ~6–8% water; 0.2% TiO₂ delustrant (this is why the fibres are white); ~1% oil agent; inner plug-wrap paper + polyvinyl acetate adhesive; outer tipping paper. Degree of acetate substitution ~2.45. Triacetin and the PVAc glue are the bits that will bite a re-melt/compounding route (plasticiser loss and a second, incompatible polymer) — the literature's preferred recovery is therefore solvent (acetone/ethyl acetate) dissolution–precipitation, not melt compounding.

  • Realistic recovery yield: a 2025 study recovered 93.9% CA from 3,000 butts (425 g → 399 g) via a chemical route — i.e. the chemistry is not the bottleneck. TerraCycle's mechanical route gets ~50%.

  • Minimum viable batch: TerraCycle told Libération (2018) it was stockpiling French and European butts because large-scale recycling needs runs of "several tens of tonnes". A real constraint for any process design.

  • Other programmes' economics for comparison: Vancouver paid $5/lb to United We Can (vs $1/lb US); Caesars Windsor generated $3,500 on 5.9 M butts (≈ $0.59/lb — inconsistent with a flat $1/lb, so Canadian rates differ or the counts are loose).

  • Named partners with volumes (TerraCycle case-study deck, c.2023): Carnival 42.5 M butts / 23 vessels / 3,500+ shipments; Chumash Casino ~29 M since 2013; Caesars Windsor 5.9 M; Disney Fantasy 740 k on one vessel; Amazon 2 M since Earth Day 2021 across a sample of 278 FCs; Keep New Hanover ~1.8 M. Largest single channel by far is cruise ships — worth knowing if the project wants a concentrated feedstock.

  • Sponsor investment: Reynolds says > $12.5 M since 2009 (reynoldsamerican.com, live 2026); a 2026 third-party piece says > $15 M and that SFNTC funded 447 receptacles in 2023 alone. Believe the company's own site; treat $15 M as the loose one.

Net effect on the project

  1. M-06 row 1 — order is now settled (whole-stream gamma first); dose/dwell still absent by design, and now demonstrably withheld rather than merely unfound. Patents confirm nothing to mine.

  2. M-06 row 5 — real mass is ~52 t, not 118 t; divisor error of 2.3× is now proven three ways.

  3. New row worth adding — capture rate ≈ 0.016%. This is the number that should drive invention-phase priorities, and it says the hard problem is collection, not processing.

  4. Two dead leads closed — the Jan Dell suit (wrong stream entirely) and the Reg A+ circular (no parameters). Both were top-ranked in the original brief; neither needs another attempt.
